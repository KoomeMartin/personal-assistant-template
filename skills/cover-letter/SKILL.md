# Cover letter

1. **Read the brief.** The posting or call (message, `inbox/`, or `vault/opportunities/`). Save it as an `opportunity` note if it isn't one, with its deadline in `calendar.md`. Note who will read the letter and what they need.
2. **Gather verified evidence only.** Read `me/cv.md`, `me/preferences.md`, `memory.md`, then the matching notes in `vault/projects/`, `vault/courses/` and `vault/documents/`. List each fact you may use with its source note. Anything not in the files is not usable; ask for it instead.
3. **Choose one argument.** In one sentence: the specific contribution the person can make to this reader's need, and the two or three strongest pieces of evidence for it. Drop everything else, however impressive. A letter is an argument, not a tour of the CV.
4. **Draft** 300–400 words (or the stated limit) in the language and spelling from `me/preferences.md`:
   - open with the contribution, not with "I am writing to apply";
   - each paragraph advances the argument and links clearly to the one before;
   - introduce a project by what it does before naming it;
   - keep team work credited as team work and metrics scoped as in the source.
5. **Review twice before showing it.**
   - Evidence pass: every claim traces to a note; nothing inflated, no invented dates, grades or skills.
   - Style pass: the person's preferences and `memory.md` lessons; cut filler, clichés and words they avoid.
6. Save to `me/applications/<organisation>-<role>-letter.md`. Reply with the letter, the source notes behind each claim, and any gaps. End with: "Read it aloud once and check every claim is true before you send it."
